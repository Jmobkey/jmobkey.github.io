What the field taught me that the courses didn't.

A replacement part that almost matches is worse than no part at all, because someone will fit it. Nearly right, in a mechanical room, is a callback at best.

Most of the design work in an AI system is deciding which parts can be probabilistic and which have to stay deterministic. That isn't a modelling skill. It comes from having been the person holding the wrong part at seven in the morning.

The second thing is who the user actually is. A contractor on a roof with gloves on and one bar of signal is not the user most enterprise software is designed for, and a great deal of it quietly dies on that gap. I spent six years being that user.

The third is that adoption is the whole game. Technology was never the hard part of getting AI into a distribution business. Data access, process reality and ownership were, and they still are.
