Make bills the modules,not the run.

Every module that fires is one operation. So an iterator over fifty records doesn't cost one operation, it costs fifty times everything downstream of it — and a polling trigger bills you for looking even when there's nothing there.

Trigger

A scheduled scenario runs whether or not there is anything to do, and the trigger module bills on every check. That line is invisible on the canvas and is routinely the largest single cost in a low-volume automation.

Scenario shape

Count every module that executes. Routers and filters are free; iterators and aggregators are not. Include the trigger in the main path count.

Reality

Error handlers, retries and the traffic your team generates testing the thing. All of it meters.

What's missing

Operations are the easiest cost to count and rarely the one that hurts. The bigger number is usually what the automation is calling: a scenario that meters 40,000 operations might be making 40,000 API calls against a service with its own per-call pricing, its own rate limits, and its own opinion about being hammered every fifteen minutes.

Also absent: data transfer on plans that meter it, the cost of a scenario sitting in an error state over a long weekend, and the engineering time to build and maintain the thing — which for anything business-critical will exceed the subscription many times over.

Two modelling choices worth knowing about. An empty poll is counted as exactly one operation, which is right for a trigger that returns nothing but understates any scenario doing work before its filter. And development traffic is applied as a flat percentage of production, when in practice it's front-loaded and lumpy.

The point isn't the operation count. It's that the number on the canvas and the number on the invoice are different numbers, and only the first one gets used to decide whether to build.
