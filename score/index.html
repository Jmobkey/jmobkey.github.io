How to score

Each one runs from 1 to 5. Be harsher than feels polite.

What's missing

Every score here is a judgement, and five judgements added together look far more precise than they are. Use this to start the argument about the order rather than to end it. The useful moment is when someone disagrees with a score, because that is usually the assumption that was about to cost something.

Two choices are built in. Readiness is the lower of data and ownership rather than their average, on the reasoning that excellent data can't make up for a missing owner, or the reverse. And risk and effort shave the score rather than divide it, so a risky idea is penalised without being wiped out. The thresholds for the call are arbitrary and meant to be argued with.

Not modelled: strategic fit, politics, dependencies between ideas, and the cost of doing nothing. The sample ideas are generic examples and don't describe any particular company.

The point isn't the score. It's that the idea easiest to pitch is often the one least ready to be built.
